Every chunk of a book can carry a vocabulary line: the words it is made of,
each with its romanisation and its meaning. **Vocabulary** puts all of those
words in one list — the words of this chapter, of the whole book, or of a
video — each word once, with what it means and how many times it comes. From
the list you can go to each place a word is used, copy the list, print it,
test yourself on it, or make a card of each word in turn.

Everything on this page is the same in a book and in a video; where they
differ, it says so.

## Opening the list

- **On a computer.** Press **vocabulary** in the header, beside **⚑ later**,
  or press the **V** key. The list opens on the right, and the page stays as it
  was beside it: nothing is darkened, and you can go on reading. A video's
  header is full in a window narrower than about 1500 pixels, so there the
  button is in the ⚙ panel instead, under **Interface**; **V** works everywhere.
- **On a phone.** Open the ⚙ panel, then **Interface**, and press
  **Vocabulary**. The list comes up from the foot of the screen.

**Esc**, the **✕** at the top of the list, or **V** again closes it; on a phone, so
does the back gesture. Review later and the contents use the same place on
the right, so opening one of them closes the others.

## What it lists

At the top, two buttons choose what the list is made of:

- **This chapter / Whole book.** In a book, *This chapter* is the chapter you
  are reading — where your reading place is — and *Whole book* is every
  chapter, including the ones the page has not loaded yet. A book of one
  chapter has only *Whole book*.
- **Whole video / Up to here.** In a video, *Up to here* is the words of the
  captions up to where the video is now: what you have already heard.

Each row is one word:

- **The word** in its own script — a verb with its other forms, as the book
  prints it (*دیدن didan · pres. بین bin · past دید did*), with the
  language's own names for them. A right-to-left word stands at the right of its row.
- **Its romanisation**, in italics, where the line gives one.
- **×3**: how many times the word is in the vocabulary lines listed.
- **Its meaning**, under it. A word given different meanings in different
  places shows each meaning once: *to see · to meet*.

**The same word, written the same way, is one row.** Capitals do not count, nor do
vowel marks, nor the half-space of Persian, nor the two ways of typing the Persian
letters ی and ک on an Arabic keyboard: *كتاب* and *کِتاب* are one word. A verb is
listed under its first form, the one the line names it by.

**What counts as a word** is what the vocabulary line marks as one: each word,
verb or compound written with the vocabulary buttons (`\dw`, `\vb`, `\bw`), and a
word (`\pw`) that starts the line or comes after a `;`. A word mentioned inside
another word's meaning — *fox, pl. ثعالب* — stays in that meaning. The meaning
is everything after the word up to the next `;`.

A video whose vocabulary lines are plain text, written before the vocabulary
buttons existed, is read by the shape of its lines: *word romanisation
meaning; next word …*, a verb as *دیدن didan · pres. بین bin · past دید did ·
to see*.

- **A language with its own script.** In Persian, Arabic, Hindi, Japanese
  and Chinese, the word is what is written in that script at the start of each
  part of the line, and the romanisation is the Latin after it, in brackets,
  in italics or bare. A bare Latin word counts as the romanisation only when
  something shows it is one: letters like *ā* or *š*, the chunk's own
  transliteration, or, in Persian and Arabic, the word's own consonants.
  Otherwise it is the start of the meaning.
- **A language written in Latin letters.** In Italian, French, German,
  Spanish, Catalan, Turkish and English, the word cannot be told from its
  meaning by its letters. It is taken where the line shows where it ends:
  the first words of the part that the chunk's own text holds, or the words
  before a dash, a colon, an equals sign, a middle dot or a bracket. A
  pronunciation after it is shown when it is in brackets, in italics or in
  IPA.
- **What it cannot read, it does not guess.** A part of a line that does not
  start with a word — *colloquial …*, or *ciao hello* beside *Buongiorno* — is
  left out, and a line that gives no word at all is counted: the line under
  the buttons says *2 lines could not be read*. A line that starts with `//`
  is a note, and is left out without being counted.

A word written in a plain line and the same word written with the buttons
are one row.

## Going to a word

Click a word: the page goes to the first place it is used and lights the
chunk for a moment. Click it again for the next place, and again for the one
after; after the last place it starts again from the first. The line under
the buttons says which place you are at, *place 2 of 5*. In a book, a place in
a chapter that is not loaded yet is loaded first; your reading place moves
with you. In a video, the video goes to the start of that caption.

On a computer the list stays open while you do this. On a phone it goes down
to show you the place; open it again and the next tap goes to the next place.

## Order and filter

- **As they come.** In the order the words first appear. This is how the
  list opens.
- **A–Z.** In the alphabet of the language — Persian in the Persian order,
  Turkish with ç after c and ı before i. Chinese is ordered by its pinyin.
  Japanese is ordered by the romanisation the line gives, since kanji have
  no order of their own.
- **Most frequent.** The words that come most often first.

The **Filter** box keeps only the words whose word, romanisation or meaning
contains what you type.

## What you can do with the list

The buttons act on the words the list is showing, in the order it shows them,
filter included.

- **Test myself.** Hides every meaning behind a **show the meaning** button.
  Read the word, say what it means, then press to check. Press **Test myself**
  again to show them all.
- **Copy.** Puts the list on the clipboard as a table: one word per line, the
  word, its romanisation and its meaning separated by tabs. Paste it into a
  spreadsheet, a document or an import box.
- **Print.** Prints the list alone, as a table, without the rest of the page.
  In *Test myself* the meanings are left out, so the sheet tests you on paper.
- **Make cards one after another.** Opens the card sheet for the first word,
  with its romanisation and meaning already in place and the chunk it comes
  from as the context. A strip at the top says *item 1 of 12*, with **Skip**
  and **Stop**. Save the card and the next word opens; **Skip** goes to the
  next without a card; **Stop**, or closing the sheet, ends it. This button is
  on a computer only: a phone never makes cards
  ([Browser and Mobile](../getting-started/mobile-mode.md)). See
  [Making a card](../cards-and-anki/making-a-card.md) for the sheet itself.

## What it does not do

The list is read from the book or the video each time you open it, and is
kept nowhere: nothing is written into the book, and there is nothing to
delete. It does not yet know which words you already know, and there is no
list across every book; both are planned. It works in every book's reader,
however long ago the book was built: nothing needs to be built again.
