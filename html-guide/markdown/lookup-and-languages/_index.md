This section has two halves, and they meet in the same place: the
language of what you are reading.

**The first half is reading help.** A book or a video can be read long
before its glosses are written, and for those weeks most chunks are phrases
you cannot get past. One page, **Reading help** in Settings, sets up everything that helps
with that: a dictionary for each language, the components of a Japanese or
Chinese character, sentences somebody has already translated, a translation
model that runs inside the page, and a small table of English synonyms that
sharpens what the model shows. All of it is optional, all of it is off
until you turn it on, and none of it is ever written into a book or a
video as if it were a gloss.

**The second half is the languages themselves.** Parseh teaches eleven:
Persian, Arabic, Italian, Japanese, French, German, Turkish, English, Hindi,
Spanish and Chinese. They share every door, button and file format; what
differs is what each language needs in order to be read — its direction,
its script, its fonts, its passes, its transliteration.

   sentence and its real translation, chosen because it shares the rare

5. [A translation model in the page](translation-model.md) — the whole

6. [Synonyms for the machine's reading](synonyms.md) — how the mark finds
   *begin* where the dictionary said *start*.

And, separately, [linguistic analysis](linguistic-analysis.md): optional analyzers
on your computer, for the dictionary panel, new phrases and the gloss prompts.

## Speech to text

12. [Speech to text](speech-to-text.md) — an optional program and two Whisper
    models that make a transcript on this computer while you add a video:
    the CPU that always works, an NVIDIA graphics card when Parseh can prove
    it is usable, what each part costs, who may get it, and where the files
    are. It has a door of its own in Settings, and is not a part of Reading
    help.

> **Nothing here is required.** A reader with no dictionary, no corpus and
> no model is exactly the reader it always was: it asks the server once
> whether anything is installed, and when nothing is, it draws no extra
> button and makes no further request.
