Backups at a glance

Parseh keeps four shelves of your own, and each has a pair of buttons on its own page: one that downloads the whole shelf as a single zip, and one that puts such a zip back. Beside them, Settings → Backups has two more: a backup of your settings, and one backup of everything for moving to a new computer. Beside every Backup button is the day it was last pressed — last backup: 3 days ago, or never backed up.

1. The four shelves#

ShelfWhere the buttons areBack it up
Booksthe books’ library, /books/, panel ⇩ The whole shelfBackup every book
Videosthe video index, /youtube/, panel ⇩ The whole shelfBackup every video
Studio documentsthe studio’s library, /studio/, in the bar’s Backup ▾ menuBackup the whole library
Exercise decksthe exercise decks, /exercises/, at the topBackup

Beside each of them, on every shelf, is Load from backup, which puts such a zip back. Settings → Backups lists the four again, each with its Backup button and its day, so that one page says what was backed up and when.

A backup is an ordinary download: the browser saves it where it saves everything. While the server packs it — a shelf of narrated books can take a while — the Working… indicator says so, on that page and on the hub, and it does the same while a backup goes back up.

The day is counted when the backup has been sent whole, not when the button is pressed: a download that failed half way is not a backup. The days are kept by the computer Parseh runs on, so every device shows the same ones.

2. Settings → Backups#

Your settings#

Backup your settings downloads what Parseh keeps for you in its config/ folder, and that no shelf holds:

Reading places, flags, levels.
Where you were in each book, the chunks you flagged to review later, the settings that follow you from device to device (the speed, the pause, the theme…) and the names you gave the levels.
Your prompts.
The ones you wrote for a chatbot.
Your languages.
The languages you added, with their files: the LaTeX file a book in that language is built with, its conventions and its lookup rules.
Your LaTeX themes.
With the default one and how long a drawing may take.
Linguistic analysis.
Where the optional analyzers are used.
Speech to text.
Which model for which language, and the checks for suspect words. The models themselves are fetched again on the new computer.

Left out, because they belong to the computer and not to you: who may reach Parseh, its port, its certificate and the devices you let in with their tokens (a new computer decides its own, and a phone is let in there once more); what phones keep, which is written by this computer’s own paths; what the updater last found; the paths of this computer’s own model for LM likelihood; the LLM connection of a0.6.2 and before, no longer read; and the days of this computer’s own backups. The page lists them too, under What is left out, and why.

Load from backup merges; it never overwrites without asking. Choosing a settings backup first shows, store by store, what loading it would do, and writes nothing:

Added.
What the backup has and this computer has not comes in.
The same.
What both have alike is left alone.
Decided by the later change.
A reading place, a setting, a level’s name: the one changed last wins, as between two of your devices — a place you reached here after the backup was made stays.
Flags to review later.
They are joined, and a removal is remembered: a flag you removed here after the backup was made stays removed, and one the backup had removed goes.
Your choice.
A prompt, a language, a LaTeX theme or a choice that differs and has no such rule is offered with keep yours and take the backup’s; yours is kept unless you pick the backup’s.

Press Load these to load it, or Cancel. A prompt whose name you have used already for another comes in as name (2). A language Parseh itself now has is left out, and said: Parseh’s own is the one read.

Everything, for a new computer#

Backup everything downloads one zip holding the four shelves’ own backups, your settings, the Anki card store and the clip tray — the two that no shelf holds: a card that never reached Anki, or a clip not yet on a card, exists nowhere else. Of the card store, the decks Parseh built for Anki and the files dropped in its inbox are left out: they are made again, or are copies of something you have.

On the new computer, install Parseh, open Settings → Backups and press Load everything from a backup…. Your settings go back first — a book, a deck or a card in a language you added needs that language — then the books, the videos, the studio, the decks, the cards and the clips, each through its own Load from backup. The answer says, part by part, what was put back and what was already there.

What is already there is kept and named, as on every shelf, unless Replace is ticked. Your settings are merged either way, and those that differ with no rule of their own are kept as they are and listed under Your settings, to take the backup’s for any of them.

A backup is checked whole before anything is put back. Every file in it is listed with its checksum; a file that does not match, a store that does not read, or a backup made by a newer Parseh is refused in words, and nothing at all is loaded.

Loading a backup is done on the computer Parseh runs on. Another device that has been let in sees the page, the days and the Backup buttons, and a lock where the Load buttons are.

3. What each one holds#

BackupIt holdsIt leaves out
Booksone bundle per book, each the book’s all of it zip: its text and chapters, its notes, its recordings in audio/ with their timings and alignmentits reader and its PDF, which build makes again
Videosone bundle per video: its glossed transcript, its notes, and a film of your own with itnothing: a YouTube video never had a file here, only its address
Studio documentsevery document with its record — its id, its tags, its dates — its pictures and recordings, and its built PDFnothing
Exercise decksevery deck with its own id, its settings, every picture and recording it has, and its scheduling — every answer you ever gavenothing
Your settingsthe six stores listed above, and your languages’ fileswhat belongs to the computer, listed above
Everythingall five above, the Anki card store and the clip traywhat each of them leaves out

A book comes back with its recordings, in the backup of everything too: every recording in its audio/ folder, its timings, its alignment review. A narration is hundreds of megabytes, so a shelf of narrated books makes a large backup. A book that came back is built from its card on the library page — build — like any book put back; the recording then plays, aligned as before.

Parseh packs a backup inside its own folder, in .tmp/, and never in the computer’s temporary folder, which on many computers is small and held in memory. The file is gone again once the backup has been sent or loaded, and anything a stopped Parseh left there is cleared the next time it starts, once it is a day old. Make sure the disk Parseh is on has room for the backup a second time while it is being made.

A shelf of books or of videos is a zip of zips: each inside is the very bundle one book or one video downloads as, so a backup can be taken apart and a single book put back by itself, through the Bring a book back panel beside the shelf’s (Bring a video back for a video).

4. Putting a shelf back#

What is already there is kept, and named. Loading a backup never quietly overwrites something newer: a book, a video, a document or a deck that is already on the shelf is left as it is, the page says which (the studio, how many), and only then offers to replace it — Replace them or Keep mine, on every shelf. A backup is usually older than what you have, and a restore of a shelf is not a merge.

It comes back as itself. A document keeps its id and the links that point at it, a deck keeps its scheduling, so what you restore carries on where it was. In the studio, where a link names the document it leads to, two documents cannot share a name: a document coming back under a name another one already has opens This name is already in use. Rename the one already there, the one coming back, or both, and press Use these names — every link to a renamed document follows it to its new name — or Cancel, and nothing is restored. (Loading everything cannot ask: the studio’s part is then refused and named, and the studio’s own Load from backup settles the names.)

An exercise deck’s backup is not its Export. A deck’s own Export ▾, on its page (or in the ⋯ menu of its card on the decks page), is for giving a deck away: Without scheduling leaves the answers behind so that whoever gets it starts new, and it carries only the pictures and recordings its exercises name. The backup is for keeping: the deck comes back as the same deck, with every file it had and every answer.

5. What no backup holds#

  • The dictionaries, sentences and models fetched on Settings → Reading help: fetch them again there.
  • Who may reach Parseh and the devices you let in, config/network.json: a Parseh on another computer starts with its own, and a phone is let in there once more.
  • The TeX packages Parseh got, texmf/: get them again on Settings → LaTeX drawings.
  • Speech to text’s program and models, stt/: gigabytes of other people’s work; get them again on Settings → Speech to text.
  • The ARASAAC pictograms, arasaac/: some 160 MB of other people’s pictures, under a licence that asks for a credit; get them again on Settings → Pictograms (ARASAAC).
  • The optional analyzers, analysis/: get them again on Settings → Linguistic analysis; your choice of where they are used is in your settings’ backup.
  • What each browser remembers: the language, the sizes and the levels shown, the page zoom.
  • What can be built again: the readers, the library page, the PDFs of the books, the LaTeX drawings (markdown/latex/), made again from their blocks.

None of it is needed to update Parseh: an update from Settings keeps every shelf, and everything in this list, as it is. Where everything lives has the folders all of these are kept in.
