Reference and troubleshooting

The other sections of this guide walk through Parseh one door at a time. This one is for looking things up: you arrive here with a question — a message on a page you do not understand, a word the other pages use without explaining it, a file you want to find on the disk — and leave with the answer.

1. Which page answers what#

When something looks wrong
— a message a page showed you, or something that did not happen: what it means and what to do. Arranged by where you met it — starting Parseh, updating it, the guide, the books, the videos, the cards, the reading help, the studio, the exercises.
Commands: install, serve, build
— the installers, the server, the book builds, the two guides and the tests, every flag.
Commands: content, lookup, Anki
— the tools behind the reading-help downloads, the book and video pipelines, the bundles, the Anki sync and the studio’s PDF.
The file layout
— the folders of Parseh, and what a book, a video, a studio document, a note, an exercise deck and an Anki deck look like on the disk.
The daily loops
— the handful of routines most days are made of, a few steps each.
Glossary
— chunk, gloss, seam, run, pass, shape, jolly card and the rest: the words Parseh uses, each in a sentence or two.
What changed, version by version
— every version, newest first, and what it brought, each thing with a line on what it does and a link to the pages that explain it.

Almost everything is done from the pages, with a button. The few things that are not — a fresh certificate, a TeX package for large print, a book or a video put back from the trash — are on the troubleshooting page, where each comes up, with what to do by hand. The two command pages are for those who like a terminal, or who are mending something by hand.

2. Two things worth knowing first#

The page says why. When Parseh cannot do something — a recording that cannot be cut, a PDF that is behind its text, a name that is already taken — it says so on the page, in a note under the greyed-out button, in a message, in a badge or in a dialog, in a full sentence. That sentence is the best first clue; the troubleshooting page quotes the common ones.

What you read is not in Parseh. The books, the videos, the studio’s documents, your exercise decks and your Anki cards are yours: a release ships the doors and none of the content. That is why a fresh install opens on empty shelves; The file layout says what a release carries, what git keeps, and what neither ever sees.

  • TroubleshootingThe messages the pages show, what each one means, and what to do about it — from starting Parseh to studying a deck.
  • Commands: install, serve, buildFor those who like a terminal — the installers, the server, the book builds, the two guides and the tests, with every flag.
  • Commands: content, lookup, AnkiThe tools behind the reading-help downloads, the book and video pipelines, the bundles, the Anki sync and the studio's PDF, with their flags.
  • File layoutThe folders of Parseh, your settings in config/, and what a book, a video, a studio document, a note, an exercise deck and an Anki deck look like on the disk.
  • The daily loopsThe routines most days are made of — reading, watching, making cards, syncing with Anki, studying, writing, adding — each in a few steps.
  • GlossaryThe words Parseh uses — chunk, gloss, seam, run, level, shape, jolly card and the rest — each in a sentence or two.
  • What’s newEvery version of Parseh, newest first, and what each one brought — a line or two on each thing, and where it is explained in full.
  • LicencesWhat Parseh is under, and whose work it carries and downloads — the fonts, MathJax, the hyphenation patterns, the dictionaries, sentences and translation models — under which licence.
