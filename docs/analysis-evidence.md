## What `analysis` is

A chunk to do may carry `analysis`: what a linguistic analyzer, a program with no
understanding of this text, read in its words: `w` is the word, `lemma` and `pos` what
it took it for, `feats` its grammar, `part_of` a word elsewhere it belongs to, and
`others` or `readings` what else the word can be. `by` says who chose: `sentence` means
the analyzer read the word in its sentence, `word alone` means it did not choose at all.

1. It is evidence, and it can be wrong. When it disagrees with what the sentence
   plainly says, the sentence is right. Use it where it settles what you were unsure of;
   ignore it where it does not.
2. It gives you no right to change `fa` or the chunks, and no reading in it may be
   written into `fa`, vowelled or otherwise.
3. It is not part of your answer: `analysis` is not a field you fill, and does not come
   back. Every rule above and below holds as if it were not there.
